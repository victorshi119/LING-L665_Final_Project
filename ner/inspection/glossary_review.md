## DISORDER (28 concepts)

A disease, disorder, or clinical motor sign: dysarthria and its subtypes, cerebral palsy and its subtypes, other neurological conditions that cause dysarthria (Parkinson's disease, ALS, stroke, ...), and related speech, language, and swallowing disorders.

| Term | Also | Definition | Mentions | Flag |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| **dysarthria** | dysarthrias | A motor speech disorder in which weakness, slowness, or poor coordination of the speech muscles, caused by damage to the central or peripheral nervous system, impairs respiration, phonation, resonance, articulation, and/or prosody, while language itself is intact. | 4801 |  |
| **spastic dysarthria** |  | Dysarthria caused by bilateral upper motor neuron damage, typically with strained-strangled voice, slow rate, hypernasality, and imprecise consonants; the dysarthria type most often associated with spastic cerebral palsy. | 75 |  |
| **flaccid dysarthria** |  | Dysarthria caused by lower motor neuron damage (cranial or spinal nerves), characterized by weakness, breathiness, hypernasality, and nasal emission. | 74 |  |
| **ataxic dysarthria** |  | Dysarthria caused by cerebellar damage, with irregular articulatory breakdowns, excess and equal stress, and poorly coordinated, scanning speech. | 58 |  |
| **hypokinetic dysarthria** |  | Dysarthria associated with basal ganglia control-circuit damage, typically in Parkinson's disease, with reduced loudness, monopitch, short rushes of speech, and imprecise consonants. | 96 |  |
| **hyperkinetic dysarthria** |  | Dysarthria caused by involuntary movements from basal ganglia disorders (e.g., chorea, dystonia), producing variable rate, loudness, and articulation. | 69 |  |
| **mixed dysarthria** |  | Dysarthria combining features of two or more single dysarthria types, as in ALS (spastic-flaccid) or multiple sclerosis (ataxic-spastic). | 51 |  |

| **dyskinetic cerebral palsy** | athetoid cerebral palsy; dyskinetic CP; athetoid CP | A subtype of cerebral palsy with involuntary, uncontrolled movements (athetosis, chorea, dystonia) caused mainly by basal ganglia injury. | 6 |  |

| **multiple sclerosis** | MS | An autoimmune disease in which demyelination of the central nervous system causes variable neurological symptoms, including ataxic and spastic dysarthria. | 164 |  |
| **stroke** | cerebrovascular accident | Sudden loss of neurological function caused by interrupted blood supply (ischemia) or bleeding in the brain; a frequent cause of acquired dysarthria and aphasia in adults. | 570 |  |

| **apraxia of speech** | acquired apraxia of speech; verbal apraxia; AOS | A motor speech disorder in which the planning or programming of speech movements is impaired, without weakness of the speech muscles. | 174 |  |

| **motor speech disorder** | motor speech impairment; MSD | A speech disorder caused by impaired planning, programming, control, or execution of speech movements; the two main types are dysarthria and apraxia of speech. | 608 |  |
| **aphasia** |  | An acquired language disorder, usually after left-hemisphere damage, impairing the comprehension and/or production of spoken or written language; distinct from dysarthria, which affects speech motor control. | 135 |  |

| **Down syndrome** | trisomy 21 | A chromosomal disorder caused by an extra copy of chromosome 21, associated with hypotonia, intellectual disability, and frequent speech and motor speech disorders. | 33 |  |
| **Huntington's disease** | Huntington disease | An inherited neurodegenerative disease causing chorea, cognitive decline, and hyperkinetic dysarthria. | 25 |  |

| **ataxia** |  | Impaired coordination of voluntary movement, usually from cerebellar damage; in speech it produces ataxic dysarthria. | 144 | ⚑ a clinical motor sign; DISORDER includes motor signs by definition |

## SPEECH_FEATURE (22 concepts)

A perceptual or acoustic property of speech that is affected in dysarthria or used to describe it, such as intelligibility, hypernasality, speech rate, or jitter. Not the disorder itself and not the test used to measure it.

| Term | Also | Definition | Mentions | Flag |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| **speech intelligibility** | intelligibility | The degree to which a speaker's intended message is understood by a listener; the primary functional outcome measure in dysarthria. | 2446 |  |
| **hypernasality** | hypernasal speech; hypernasal resonance | Excessive nasal resonance on oral speech sounds because the velopharyngeal port does not close adequately; common in spastic and flaccid dysarthria. | 219 |  |
| **imprecise consonants** | imprecise articulation | Consonants produced with reduced accuracy or distorted place or manner of articulation; one of the most frequent perceptual features across dysarthria types. | 94 |  |
| **speech rate** | speaking rate; rate of speech; articulation rate | How fast a person speaks, usually measured in syllables or words per unit time (articulation rate excludes pauses); typically reduced in spastic dysarthria. | 450 |  |
| **prosody** |  | The rhythm, stress, and intonation patterns of speech, carried by variations in pitch, loudness, and duration; often reduced or disrupted in dysarthria. | 503 |  |
| **monopitch** |  | Reduced pitch variation, making speech sound flat; a characteristic of hypokinetic and spastic dysarthria. | 37 |  |
| **monoloudness** |  | Reduced variation in loudness across an utterance. | 36 |  |
| **breathiness** | breathy voice | A voice quality with audible air escape caused by incomplete vocal fold closure; typical of flaccid dysarthria. | 54 |  |
| **harshness** | harsh voice; strained-strangled voice; strained voice | A rough, effortful voice quality caused by excessive laryngeal tension; strained-strangled voice is a hallmark of spastic dysarthria. | 57 |  |
| **voice quality** |  | The characteristic sound of a speaker's voice apart from pitch and loudness, shaped by phonation and resonance (e.g., breathy, harsh, hoarse). | 196 |  |
| **fundamental frequency** | F0 | The rate of vocal fold vibration, the main acoustic correlate of perceived pitch; its mean and variability are standard acoustic measures in dysarthria. | 398 |  |
| **formant** | formant frequency | A resonance peak of the vocal tract (F1, F2, ...) that determines vowel quality; formant patterns reflect tongue and jaw movement. | 308 |  |

| **jitter** |  | Cycle-to-cycle variation in the period of vocal fold vibration (frequency perturbation); a measure of voice instability. | 115 |  |
| **shimmer** |  | Cycle-to-cycle variation in the amplitude of vocal fold vibration (amplitude perturbation). | 92 |  |
| **harmonics-to-noise ratio** | harmonic-to-noise ratio; HNR | The ratio of periodic (harmonic) to aperiodic (noise) energy in the voice signal; lower values indicate a breathier or hoarser voice. | 73 |  |
| **diadochokinetic rate** | diadochokinesis; alternating motion rate; sequential motion rate; DDK | The speed and regularity of rapidly repeated syllables such as /pa/, /ta/, /ka/ or /pataka/; used to assess oral motor speech control. | 183 | ⚑ a measurement from a standard task; SPEECH_FEATURE or ASSESSMENT? |
| **maximum phonation time** | MPT | The longest time a person can sustain a vowel on one breath; reflects respiratory and laryngeal efficiency. | 121 | ⚑ a measurement from a standard task; SPEECH_FEATURE or ASSESSMENT? |
| **articulation** | speech articulation | The production of speech sounds by movements of the lips, tongue, jaw, and velum; one of the speech subsystems affected in dysarthria. | 916 |  |
| **phonation** |  | The production of voice by vibration of the vocal folds driven by exhaled air; one of the speech subsystems affected in dysarthria. | 294 |  |
| **voice onset time** | VOT | The interval between the release of a stop consonant and the onset of vocal fold vibration; distinguishes voiced from voiceless stops and is often disrupted in dysarthria. | 21 |  |
| **speech naturalness** | naturalness | How normal or typical speech sounds to listeners, independent of intelligibility; often reduced by prosodic abnormalities in dysarthria. | 341 |  |
